## The idea

**Ice-Harvest Bench:** a bounded, bench-scale pilot for extracting water from simulated cold-trap regolith, designed entirely under salvage-first doctrine and ledgered by hand.

## Problem statement (bounded)

Extract at least **100 g of water per day-equivalent** from a terrestrial analog of lunar cold-trap regolith (e.g., ice-doped granular material at dry-ice temperatures), under a hard **500 W continuous power cap**, using salvaged or scrap-grade components wherever function allows, with a **hand-maintained mass/energy budget ledger** (paper or plain text; no software required to keep it alive).

This is a design problem, not a build commitment: the deliverable is a short design document, not hardware. Domain context: Astroid-miner notebooks (thermal extraction, mass/energy budgets); LazarusForge doctrine (salvage-first gate hierarchy, evidence labels).

## Constraints

1. 500 W continuous cap — hard.
2. Salvage-first gate mapping required for every major component (function -> assembly -> component -> material, per LazarusForge doctrine).
3. Ledger must remain maintainable by a human with no software dependency.
4. Significant claims carry evidence labels (Measured / Replicated / Simulated / Analogous / Placeholder). No claim of demonstrated capability — this is design only.
5. Top failure modes named, not implied.

## Deliverable

A design document of at most two pages: concept, mass/energy budget table, salvage gate map, top three failure modes, evidence labels, open unknowns.
